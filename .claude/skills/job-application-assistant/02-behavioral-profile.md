# Behavioral Profile

*Note: No formal assessment (PI, DISC, Myers-Briggs, etc.) completed. Profile below is inferred from conversation — review before relying on it in high-stakes applications.*

## Overview
Selim is an **adaptable generalist**. His explicit preference is to shape his profile to fit the specific role rather than committing to a single career direction. He is energised by building complete, working systems and solving concrete problems end-to-end.

## Core Behavioral Drives

| Drive | Level | Meaning |
|-------|-------|---------|
| Adaptability | High | Actively adjusts framing, stack, and emphasis to fit the role; maintains three distinct CV variants as evidence |
| Builder orientation | High | Motivated by shipping complete, production-quality software — projects consistently go from idea to deployment |
| Initiative | High | Personal projects (EV tracker, election engine) show self-directed work beyond course requirements |
| Breadth preference | High | Comfortable spanning infrastructure, backend, mobile, and web; explicitly dislikes being siloed into pure frontend |

## Strongest Behaviors
- **Adaptable:** Explicitly prefers to shape to the role. Has demonstrated this across three distinct technical directions (cloud/DevOps, mobile, general software engineering).
- **End-to-end ownership:** Projects consistently go from idea to production deployment (EV tracker on Vercel, Fake ID app shipped for internship, election engine from data scraping to MIP model).
- **Initiative beyond requirements:** LLM-powered Ansible automation at Intertech was a proactive engineering choice, not a specified task in the intern/part-time scope.

## How You Work Best
- With clear goals and ownership of a defined scope
- Across the full stack — discomfort with being purely frontend
- In environments that reward shipping over process

## Growth Areas (frame positively in applications)
- **Depth vs breadth trade-off:** Generalist background means no single deep specialisation yet. Frame as: "rapidly acquires depth in whichever area the role demands; my breadth means I understand the systems around my specialism."
- **Unclear requirements:** Explicitly named as a drain. Frame as: "works best with well-defined scope; when requirements are ambiguous, I bring structure by proposing clear deliverables early."

## Mapping to Job Posting Language

When a job posting mentions these keywords, it's a **strong behavioral fit**:
- "full-stack", "end-to-end", "ownership"
- "autonomous", "self-directed", "initiative", "proactive"
- "fast-paced", "ship quickly", "startup mindset"
- "cross-functional", "wear many hats"
- "pragmatic", "delivery-focused"

When a job posting mentions these, flag as **potential friction** (not deal-breaker):
- "deep specialist in X" — Selim can go deep but the role needs to also value breadth
- "heavy process / extensive documentation culture" — may find this slow; frame as "I follow process where it adds value"

## Management Style Preferences
- Works well with managers who set clear goals and give autonomy on implementation
- Less suited to micromanagement or highly prescriptive environments

## Using This in Applications
- **Cover letters:** Frame adaptability as a feature: "I bring a broad foundation and focus whichever part of the stack the role requires most."
- **CV:** Emphasise end-to-end project delivery; lead with what the specific role values rather than listing every technology equally
- **Interviews:** Use EV Trip Tracker (personal initiative, full-stack delivery), LLM automation at Intertech (proactive improvement, adopted by the ops team), and E-Commerce platform (team leadership, Agile delivery) as core STAR examples
- **Don't overstate:** Don't claim deep specialisation in any single area without contextualising the breadth; don't claim pure ML/research credentials
